  Two design choices worth pointing at explicitly in the pitch
  Refusing the tally. Most collaborative tools show a running count of how the room is leaning. Suppressing
  it is a deliberate anti-anchoring decision and it is exactly what the automation-bias and group-judgment

  sophisticated piece of design and no judge will expect it.

  The Gottmann and Karmaus figures measure study replicability between replicate experiments, which

   THE OPENING LINE THIS EVIDENCE LICENSES

   Three hepatologists reading the same liver injury case agree completely about a quarter of the time.
   Replicate carcinogenicity studies concur on roughly 57% of classifications. This is not a criticism of
   toxicologists; it is what happens when skilled people integrate conflicting evidence without a shared,
   recorded method. That is the problem we took on.


06    Design Evidence and the Counter-Evidence
Explanations can make over-reliance worse
Multiple studies show that adding explanations can increase uncritical acceptance, because a plausible-
sounding rationale invites agreement rather than scrutiny, and human-plus-AI teams frequently underperform the
AI alone. Cognitive forcing functions, interventions that compel analytical engagement, measurably reduced
over-reliance on incorrect AI recommendations relative to explanation-only designs, at a cost in user effort
(Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos 2021, Proc. ACM Hum.-Comput. Interact. 5, CSCW1, Article 188).

Where the build already answers this: the deliberation app's blind-position step is a cognitive forcing function
in the strongest available form. The assessor must commit a reasoned call before seeing anyone else's. Where
it does not: in apps/web the verdict renders unconditionally the moment the Case tab mounts, with no
provisional-judgment gate.

Multi-agent debate helps, but conformity propagates
Multiple model instances critiquing each other improves factual accuracy (Du et al., ICML 2024), but later work
shows persuasion can override truth and sycophancy can propagate confident errors. If the adjudicator ever



ARBITER · Evidence-Integrated Round 1 Playbook · Team BU 1                                                     Page 6 of 13
moves from stub to live, keep the human sign-off as the terminating authority — which the current design
already does.

Standards that make the output legible to a regulator
   OECD (Q)SAR Assessment Framework (Series on Testing and Assessment No. 386, 2023; second edition
   2024 adds a result-reporting format for combining multiple predictions) — the current regulatory standard for
   assessing QSAR predictions, organised around defined endpoint, applicability domain, prediction reliability,
   and fitness for purpose.
   ASME V&V40 and the FDA's November 2023 guidance on assessing the credibility of computational
   modeling, structured on model influence times decision consequence.
   CDISC SEND, required for FDA nonclinical submissions and already the donation format used by cross-
   company safety data sharing.
   W3C PROV-O for machine-readable provenance, a natural emission format for the existing hash-chained
   record.

Precedent that cross-company collaboration works
MELLODDY trained federated models across 10 pharmaceutical companies on more than 2.6 billion confidential
activity data points covering over 21 million molecules and 40,000 assays, reporting collaborative models
roughly 4% better at categorising activity with about a 10% increase in applicability domain, and all ten partners
improving (Heyndrickx et al. 2023, J. Chem. Inf. Model. 63(45)). eTOX and eTRANSAFE pooled roughly 9 million
preclinical data points from 8,196 studies across 13 companies. VICT3R, launched September 2024 with 33
partner organisations, is building shared virtual control groups to replace concurrent controls, which typically
account for around 25% of animals in a toxicity study.

The unclaimed position: those consortia share data, models, or controls. None shares an auditable reasoning
layer. A versioned rule base and argumentation standard exchanged between companies without exchanging
compounds is genuinely unprecedented. Present it as a closing vision slide anchored to those three precedents,
never as something built.

09    The Measurement Worth Running
§13.4e names Gate 0, the adjudicator consistency probe, as the one measurement to run if only one thing
gets done: about an hour, roughly a dollar, no answer key required, and the only test that can show the
architecture is wrong before more is built on it.

The repository has never had an API key, which is why the pass marks could be committed honestly before any
model was called, and why the path from a real browser through a real HTTP server to a real model has never
executed. Running the probe converts the strongest untested assumption into a number.

If you run it, honour the rule already written down: a failing flip rate is a design defect, and the response is
not to rewrite the prompt. The prompt is a model parameter, and tuning it against the test set is leakage. Also
worth remembering in the pitch: a correct verdict reached by incorrect reasoning is a failure, and chance alone
reaches roughly 70% on fifteen cases.

  The determinism contrast is the number to lead with instead of accuracy
  One thousand runs producing a single hash, verified by test, alongside an in-browser recomputation of
  every test-split verdict checked against a committed manifest. If the consistency probe shows the
  language-model adjudicator varying across identical inputs, the comparison writes itself, and it is a claim
  about the architecture rather than about accuracy — which is the only kind of claim the current numbers
  support.

11      Q&A: the Hard Ones
Five minutes with live judges. The first four questions below are the ones that could go badly. Rehearse

these until they are calm and short.

  Your balanced accuracy is 0.500. That is chance. Why are we looking at this?
  Because on the same honest target the best pipeline we tested reaches 0.601 and naive aggregation reaches 0.471.
  Nothing solves this from public evidence streams. What we can demonstrate is that the system is deterministic across
  a thousand runs, that it declines when the evidence cannot support a commitment, that it can prove which of those
  declines were structurally unavoidable, and that every commitment it did make identified a genuine BSEP inhibitor.
  Those are the claims we are making.


  It abstains 260 times out of 267. Is it useful?
  On this corpus, that is the correct answer 260 times. The evidence streams available publicly do not support a
  commitment for those compounds, and a subset could not have committed at any evidence values, which the system
  reports. The value is not the volume of verdicts; it is that the seven commitments are traceable and the 260 declines
  are explained. Inside a company with real assay data, the ratio changes.


  Five of your seven commitments are approved, widely prescribed drugs. You would have blocked
  amlodipine's peers.
  Yes, and that is our most useful finding. All five are genuine BSEP inhibitors, so the mechanism detection is right.
  What the ruleset lacks is any vocabulary for severity, so it cannot distinguish a real mechanism from a clinically
  manageable one. That is structural. No threshold moves it. The next version needs a severity axis, and we would
  rather name that than hide it behind a tuned cutoff.


  How do you know your evaluation is not contaminated by hindsight?
  For part of it, we know it is. We assumed a mechanical cut between the nonclinical and clinical sections of a review
  guaranteed blindness, and we measured that false: the Turalio nonclinical chapter contains a clinical cross-reference,
  because it was written by reviewers who already knew the outcome. Cutting at the chapter boundary moves the
  pages, not the knowledge. That compound is now a deliberation case and never a prediction case, and any future
  prediction scoring has to grep the nonclinical extract for clinical cross-references first. That check does not exist yet.


  Is the adjudicator real?
  The wiring is real; the words are a stub. There is no API key in the repository, which is why our pass marks could be
  committed before any model was called. The interface says so on screen rather than letting anyone mistake stub
  output for a judgment.


  How is this different from Lhasa's Derek or Kaptis?
  They are more mature and better at prediction, and we are not competing there. No tool in that family runs a formal
  argumentation over conflicting claims, preserves and displays the conflict measure rather than averaging it away,
  shows which single claim would flip the verdict, and routes the decision through blind multi-reviewer positions with a
  hash-chained override record.


  Why should we trust a symbolic system over a language model here?
  Determinism, and we measured it: a thousand runs, one hash. Identical evidence produces an identical verdict and an
  identical trace. Whether the language-model adjudicator does the same is exactly the consistency probe we have
  specified and not yet run, and we would not claim the comparison before running it.

     Closing
     Round 1 asks whether this can realistically create value and be implemented. The strongest evidence for
     that is not the accuracy figure, which does not support it. It is that this team built two working applications
     and roughly 938 tests, then audited its own headline result, found it invalid, published the correction, and
     re-graded to a worse number rather than a better one. A tool whose purpose is making evidence conflicts
     legible and decisions defensible has already been applied, by its authors, to itself. Say that, and mean it.
