Read this before you present the verdict

An FDA multi-disciplinary review is one document, written by reviewers who already knew the clinical outcome. Two of the nine findings below — the 39.4 per cent transaminase rate and the Hy’s Law cases — are drawn from the clinical safety section at pages 189–190. They are real evidence, and they are also hindsight.

Use this case to exercise the deliberation, never to score prediction. If someone asks whether ARBITER “predicted” tucatinib’s hepatotoxicity, the honest answer is that it was never blind to it. What the case demonstrates is the reconciliation of evidence that genuinely disagrees — and page 98 supplies that disagreement all by itself.

Nine findings, every one quoted

Six assert toxicity, three assert safety, and two of them sit on the same page of the same review contradicting each other. Each carries the verbatim sentence it was drawn from and the page it appears on.

Liver is a target organ in rat and monkey

Toxic M5 tuc-liver-target-organ

Repeat-dose studies in rats and monkeys name the liver as a target organ alongside the GI tract: clinical-pathology shifts, increased liver weight and hepatocyte hypertrophy. An adaptive-looking picture rather than necrosis, but the review treats the liver as one of the two major toxicities of the compound.

“In rats and monkeys, treatment-related liver toxicities included changes in clinical pathology, increases in liver weight and hepatocyte hypertrophy.” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 34

Rat liver findings begin at the human exposure

Toxic C1 C2 tuc-exposure-margin

In the 13-week studies the liver findings in female rats begin at an exposure similar to what a patient receives at the recommended 300 mg twice-daily dose — a margin of roughly 1×, not 10×. The monkey findings sit at about 3×. There is no exposure headroom on the rat finding.

“In the 13-week studies, liver toxicities were observed in female rats at doses ≥ 20 mg/kg/day (similar to the human exposure at the recommended dose of 300 mg twice daily based on AUC) and in monkeys at 40 mg/kg/day (approximately 3 times the exposure in humans at the recommended dose of 300 mg twice daily based on AUC).” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 34

BSEP transport inhibited 47.1 per cent at 10 µM

Toxic M2 tuc-bsep-inhibition

Direct measurement of bile-salt export pump inhibition — the most common route to cholestatic injury, and a mechanism rule R2 treats as a key event rather than a structural correlation. Nearly half of BSEP-mediated transport is inhibited at 10 micromolar. A public review almost never contains this number.

“ONT-380 inhibited the transport of the probe substrates of BCRP and BSEP by 67.7% and 47.1%, respectively.” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 98

Reviewers judged the transporter interaction risk low

Safe M2 tuc-bsep-risk-low

The same page that reports 47.1 per cent BSEP inhibition concludes the interaction likelihood is low, because 10 micromolar is far above the concentration a patient’s hepatocytes see. This finding and the one above cover the same checklist question and disagree, which leaves that question present rather than resolved.

“The regulatory authorities consider the likelihood of drug interaction with inhibitors of BCRP (tucatinib only), BSEP (tucatinib only), OAT2 (ONT-993 only) to be low.” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 98

Findings reversed in a four-week recovery period

Safe C4 tuc-reversibility

The toxicities were dose-dependent and reversible, or trending reversible, after four treatment-free weeks. This is the strongest reassuring finding in the package: a reversible, monitorable signal is managed with liver function tests rather than stopped.

“In repeat-dose toxicology studies with tucatinib, toxicities were generally dose-dependent and were reversible or showed tendency of reversibility after a 4-week treatment-free recovery period.” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 35

No disproportionate human metabolite

Safe M3 tuc-no-dispro-metabolite

Tucatinib is cleared by metabolism through the hepatobiliary route in rat and monkey, and no human metabolite appears at a level requiring its own nonclinical assessment.

“There are no human disproportionate metabolites requiring additional nonclinical studies.” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 34

Three months of animal dosing against indefinite human dosing

Toxic C6 tuc-dosing-duration

The repeat-dose programme runs to three months in both species. Patients take tucatinib twice daily until their disease progresses, routinely longer than three months, and the liver is already a target organ at three months. The duration is stated and it exceeds what was tested.

“Twice daily oral administration of tucatinib was assessed in repeat-dose toxicity studies for up to 3 months in rats and monkeys, consistent with the clinical route of administration and intended dosing schedule.” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 34

39.4 per cent of treated patients had a liver-enzyme event

Toxic C5 tuc-clinical-hepatotox

In HER2CLIMB, AST/ALT/bilirubin increases occurred in 39.4% of patients on tucatinib against 22.8% on control. Hepatotoxicity drove dose reduction in 8% of patients and discontinuation in 1.5%, and the FDA placed it under Warnings and Precautions. The population is patients with advanced metastatic disease.

“TEAEs of AST/ALT/bilirubin increase occurred in 39.4% of subjects treated with tucatinib on HER2CLIMB. The incidence was higher on the tucatinib arm than the control arm (22.8%).” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 189 — clinical safety section, see caution above

Hy’s Law cases, in a hepatocellular pattern

Toxic C3 tuc-hys-law

Eleven subjects met the broad laboratory screen of AST/ALT above 3× ULN with concurrent bilirubin above 2× ULN, and five met full Hy’s Law laboratory criteria. Hy’s Law is a hepatocellular pattern by definition, so the injury-pattern question is answered in kind. Attribution to tucatinib alone is confounded — every patient was also on capecitabine and many had liver metastases — but the pattern is not in doubt.

“A total of 11 subjects in the tucatinib integrated safety population met the initial broader laboratory criteria of AST/ALT>3xULN and concurrent bilirubin>2xULN.” FDA NDA 213411 multi-disciplinary review · p. 190 — clinical safety section, see caution above

The adjudication

Mechanism first, consequence second, and the two kept apart on purpose — a mechanism can be real and still not reach the patient.

Mechanism
Bile-salt export pump (BSEP) inhibition

Present. Cited to tuc-bsep-inhibition and tuc-bsep-risk-low — the pathway is named from the two findings that disagree about it, not from the one that suits the verdict.

Consequence

Advance

The package supports advancing because the liver findings, while significant (including clinical hepatotoxicity and Hy’s Law cases), are reversible upon withdrawal and can be managed with routine liver function monitoring, which is acceptable given the target population of advanced metastatic breast cancer.

Cited: tuc-liver-target-organ · tuc-exposure-margin · tuc-reversibility · tuc-dosing-duration · tuc-clinical-hepatotox · tuc-hys-law

Basis
  • Projected human daily dose
  • Exposure margin: tested concentration or NOAEL against projected human Cmax
  • Injury pattern: hepatocellular, cholestatic or mixed
  • Reversibility on withdrawal
  • Expected frequency and the population that would be exposed
  • Intended dosing duration

All six consequence questions were answered. The verdict rests on the complete half of the checklist, and the record says so rather than implying it.

What would change this answer

Four gaps, each named with the thing it blocks. The last one is not a missing document — it is the claim a reviewer entered from outside the package, recorded as a gap because nothing in the evidence supports it yet.

Human-cell hepatotoxicity result

Without human-cell evidence, rule R1 cannot be applied to compare human and animal in vivo sensitivity.

Mitochondrial toxicity result

Without mitochondrial toxicity data, rule R2 cannot distinguish direct pathway key events from structural correlations.

Structural alert or QSAR assessment, with applicability domain stated

Without QSAR data and its applicability domain, rule R4 cannot be applied to weight structural predictions.

External claim entered by A. Silva

“A human hepatocyte assay at clinically relevant concentrations would separate the two readings of the BSEP result.” This assay would resolve the conflict between the 47.1% BSEP inhibition finding and the low-risk assessment.

Recommended next experiment

Conduct a human hepatocyte assay at clinically relevant concentrations to resolve the BSEP inhibition risk and evaluate human-specific hepatotoxicity.

This is the part worth pausing on in a demo. The planner did not ask “which assay is usually informative?” — it asked which rule was doing the defeating and what evidence would overturn that specific rule. R3 turned on a concentration nobody in the room could pin down, so the recommendation is the one experiment that settles the disagreement between B. Mehta and C. Lindqvist, and it simultaneously fills the M1 gap that blocked R1 entirely.
